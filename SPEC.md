# Playbook v0.1 Technical Specification

## 1. Purpose

Playbook is a framework for constructing partially observable, multi-step legal work
environments for evaluation, data generation, supervised fine-tuning, preference
optimization, and reinforcement learning.

The v0.1 goal is deliberately narrow: prove that one realistic legal matter can be
represented as state, observations, actions, transitions, verifiers, rewards, and an
auditable episode trace.

## 2. Research question

Can post-training in a structured legal environment improve an open model's performance
on unseen legal matters while reducing critical errors such as fabricated citations,
unsupported assumptions, and unauthorized departures from a client playbook?

## 3. Episode model

An episode consists of:

1. `reset(seed)` initializes public state, hidden state, budgets, and the trace.
2. The agent receives a public observation.
3. The agent submits one structured action.
4. The environment validates and executes the action.
5. The environment returns a new observation and incremental reward.
6. The episode ends on `submit_final` or truncates at the step limit.
7. The environment returns a normalized score, raw score, critical-failure status,
   and criterion-level breakdown.

## 4. Observation contract

Observations are JSON-serializable dictionaries containing:

- matter metadata;
- role and assignment;
- visible document index;
- available action schemas;
- current budgets;
- facts learned through permitted actions;
- submissions already made;
- the result of the immediately preceding action;
- and warnings generated by the environment.

Hidden facts, rubric answers, counterparty fallback positions, and undisclosed document
content must never appear in observations.

## 5. Action contract

v0.1 supports:

- `read_document(document_id, section?)`
- `search_matter(query)`
- `ask_client(question_id, question)`
- `submit_issue(issue_id, title, severity, citations, analysis, recommendation)`
- `propose_redline(issue_id, document_id, section, replacement_text, rationale)`
- `submit_final(summary)`

Actions are intentionally structured. Later versions may accept natural-language tool
calls and parse them into this schema.

## 6. Reward contract

The v0.1 reward engine uses deterministic, additive criteria with explicit gates.

Positive signals include:

- asking a required, nonredundant client question;
- identifying a rubric issue;
- assigning correct severity;
- citing required provisions;
- including required reasoning concepts;
- proposing redline language containing operative concepts;
- and submitting a complete final summary.

Negative signals include:

- unsupported issue IDs;
- nonexistent document or section citations;
- redundant client questions;
- exceeding budgets;
- missing required final artifacts;
- and triggering critical-failure patterns.

A critical failure caps the normalized episode score at the configured maximum.

## 7. Scoring philosophy

Playbook does not treat all errors as interchangeable. A polished answer containing a
fabricated quotation or a reversed liability allocation can be unusable despite high
average issue recall. Rubrics therefore distinguish:

- required criteria;
- secondary criteria;
- prohibited claims;
- critical failures;
- and procedural constraints.

## 8. Determinism

Given the same matter, seed, and action sequence, v0.1 must produce the same observations,
rewards, terminal state, and trace. LLM-based simulators and graders are postponed until
the deterministic layer is tested.

## 9. Training interfaces

The episode trace is the canonical training artifact. It should support conversion to:

- supervised chat trajectories;
- chosen/rejected preference pairs;
- prompt-only rollout datasets;
- and environment-owned reward functions for online RL.

## 10. Evaluation protocol

Every experiment should report:

- normalized reward;
- raw reward;
- required-issue recall;
- unsupported-issue count;
- citation validity;
- required-question recall;
- redline completion;
- critical-error-free completion rate;
- steps and tokens, where available;
- and performance on a private held-out matter set.

## 11. Security, ethics, and provenance

Matter authors must not use confidential client materials, employer playbooks, privileged
work product, or recognizable reconstructed matters. Each matter should include a source
and provenance declaration. Synthetic documents should avoid reproducing proprietary
forms or distinctive confidential language.
