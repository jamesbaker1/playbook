# Progress record — August 8, 2026

*A dated snapshot of the day the instrument got audited. The benchmark's
critical-failure gates are the preregistered primary metric of Playbook-1, and
this is the day we stopped assuming they measure what they claim to. Written for
anyone (including future us) reconstructing what happened and why. Previous
record: `docs/progress-2026-08-07.md`; ledger context: `docs/plan-2026-08.md`;
forward plan: `docs/playbook-1-plan.md`.*

## Shipped

**The sealed corpus got a real pre-review, and it failed it** (private
repository). A 13-agent adversarial workflow — one deep reviewer per family, one
independent skeptic re-verifying every finding against the actual engine, plus
contamination, overlap, and mechanical agents — went through the five
model-drafted sealed families that stand between us and the 10-family evaluation
floor. Result: **10 blockers, every one reproduced by engine replay twice
independently**, and every one the same species of defect — a regex
critical-failure gate firing on *correct*, playbook-compliant work. Recommending
the client playbook's own fix, quoting a recital accurately and then negating it,
drafting a belt-and-braces prohibition: each collapses a 1.000 reference to the
0.25 critical cap. None of the five is approvable as-is; none needs rejection.

**The mechanical fixes are drafted and waiting on the owner**: branch
`gate-fixes-2026-08-07` in playbook-private (commits `1a2243f` + `d338f04`,
pushed, not merged) fixes all ten blockers plus the mechanically-clear majors,
ships the 20 false-firing probe sentences as a permanent `probes/` regression
suite, recomputes the five sealed digests, and adds two CI steps (probe replay,
digest-vs-disk verification). Verified: zero scoring drift on every reference and
bad trajectory; every probe critical pre-fix, clean post-fix. The judgment calls
— five families' worth — remain open and unimplemented.

**Then we pointed the same probe at the public corpus, and it is worse.**
Adversarial probing with full engine replay confirmed **84 blocker-grade and 52
major false positives across all 11 gated public matters and the variant specs**
(`clean_msa_009` ships no gates and produced none). The failure classes are
consistent: state-then-negate analysis of exactly the kind each matter's
`instructions.md` asks for ("no law prohibits all model training"; "it is not
unconscionable as a matter of law"), belt-and-braces prohibitions in the
document's own words
("**Neither** Provider **nor** Customer may use Provider Data"), savings clauses
("**nothing** in this Section restricts…"), and deletion recitals. In
`msa_provider_004` the shipped reference trajectory clears one gate only by
omitting the words "to Customer" — the reference answer sits one preposition from
a critical failure.

**The gates are also trivially dodged in the other direction.** The same probe
found silent evasions of the exact conduct each gate exists to catch: one
inserted adjective, one dropped "commercially", a swapped verb, or a word-order
change is usually enough. Two findings deserve naming:

- **Total capitulation on uncapped liability** in `msa_provider_004` — by the
  precise route the client playbook names and forbids ("the cap does not apply to
  Section X") — replays at normalized 0.9648 with `critical_failure` false.
- **The `quotes[]`-only fabrication gap.** Verbatim verification inspects the
  `quotes[]` field. The identical fabricated attribution, asserted in analysis
  prose instead, costs an agent nothing but the quote points it declines to claim
  — and is not a critical failure. This is the cheapest available reward hack in
  the environment today.

Taken together: on the primary metric, the instrument currently penalizes careful
lawyering more reliably than it catches careless lawyering.

**The engine answer shipped, dormant** (`6d8cc6a`). Rather than hand-temper 136
regexes into unreviewable blobs, gate entries may now opt into a structured form
— `pattern` plus optional `negation_guard`, `require_context`, `exclude_context`
— across all three gate sites (issue, redline, settlement) through one shared
`gate_match` helper, so the semantics cannot diverge. Sentence boundaries never
split at a period before a digit, so `§10.2` stays intact; the negator list is
closed and includes the typographic-apostrophe `n't` (a probed evasion vector).
Plain-string patterns are **byte-identical** to before, proven by replaying 12
real trajectories with scores, critical flags, and gate attributions frozen into
the test. The linter validates the mapping form and rejects unknown keys rather
than ignoring them. No shipped rubric opts in yet. Suite: **412 tests**, green.

**The public-corpus migration onto that mechanism is in flight** and lands as a
separate follow-up commit, deliberately: it changes what the instrument measures,
so it is recorded as an **explicit instrument revision**, not as a bug fix folded
into an engine change. Every row published so far was measured on the
pre-migration instrument; the migration commit is where that gets recorded, and
critical-failure rates measured after it are not comparable to the ones before.

**Two frontier reference rows landed** (`results/v0.4.0/`,
`docs/baseline-report.md`), same protocol as the v0.4.0 rows, seed 0, all 12
public matters, via a commercial gateway with output capped at 4,096 tokens:

- **GPT-5.6-terra: 0.474** — the best model score measured — with **zero critical
  failures**, citation validity 1.000, zero unsupported issues, and **30.2 steps
  per episode, more than the expert reference's 22.6** (open models: 8–11). The
  first measured model to break the critical-rate-rises-with-scale pattern, and
  the first to work the file at reference depth.
- **Claude Haiku 4.5: 0.336** with a **25% critical rate** (3 of 12) and citation
  validity 0.688 — the open-model failure archetypes intact at a much higher
  average score.
- **Question recall ≤ 0.083 for both** against the expert reference's 0.958. The
  client-interaction gap is universal: no measured model, at any scale, from any
  lab, treats fact gathering as part of the job.

**The two-teacher scaffolded rollout pilot ran and produced a candidate**
(`results/rollout-pilot-2/`). It repeats the 2026-08-06 32B pilot design
exactly — same four train-split variants, seeds 0–1, temperature 0.7, same
runner, same filter stack with the preregistered 0.5 score bar — changing only
the teachers (API models) and the system prompt (`training/scaffold_prompt.txt`):

- `deepseek/deepseek-v3.2`: **6 of 8 above the bar** (mean 0.509), including
  **both seeds of `ml_development_ip_distribution_003`**, the fabrication-trap
  variant that critically failed both 32B seeds and both Qwen3-235B seeds.
- `qwen/qwen3-235b-a22b-2507`: 2 of 8 (mean 0.378). The 2026-08-06 32B: 0 of 8.
- The scaffold worked on process: all 16 episodes read every available document
  and ran 13–30 steps (32B: 4–18); client questions appear in 5 of 8 Qwen3-235B
  episodes and all 8 DeepSeek episodes (32B: none).
- The remaining failures are genuine, not artifacts: all four critical failures
  across both teachers are verbatim-verifier catches on invented quotations, and
  the largest score losses are unsupported-issue bursts that drive raw reward
  negative before normalization clamps it to 0.0.
- Cost: $0.235 (Qwen3-235B) and $0.523 (DeepSeek) for 8 episodes each — about
  **$0.087 per surviving trajectory** at DeepSeek's yield.

**The preregistered decision rule is now tested code** (`2040ffe`), written
before any training data exists: a paired family-clustered bootstrap of the
difference in condition means, plus a contract-driven release-gate evaluator
(`playbook-analysis`) that reads every gate, comparison, allowance, and
confidence level from the frozen YAML rather than hardcoding it. Missing episode
indicators raise instead of scoring as perfect; `excludes_zero` is derived from
the rounded published bounds so the boolean can never contradict the number. 42
new tests; verified end-to-end against real v0.4.0 scorecard rows. Its one known
gap closed the same day (`9228cac`): bench rows now carry `protocol_failures`, so
the contract's per-condition protocol-failure secondary stops reporting
`available: false`. That one-line change was deliberately deferred until the
in-flight metered sweep finished, so no single scorecard mixes row schemas.

**Pilot infrastructure** (`d46348e`): `run_episode` gained a keyword-only
`system_prompt` (`None` keeps the baseline prompt byte-identical, so scorecard
rows stay comparable); the rollout generator gained `--system-prompt-file`,
`--max-tokens`, repeatable `--variant`, and per-episode sidecar checkpointing
with resume-skip, so an interrupted pilot never re-pays for a completed episode;
`training/scaffold_prompt.txt` targets each failure mode the 32B pilot documented;
`training/score_pilot.py` reproduces the prior pilot's filter stages and adds the
scaffold-compliance diagnostics (`documents_read`, `read_every_document`,
`questions_asked`, `escalations_raised`) that made today's comparison possible.

## The through-line

Yesterday the environment stopped being unmeasured. Today the *measuring
instrument* stopped being unexamined — and it did not survive contact with an
adversary: 84 public blockers, 10 sealed ones, and a fabrication gate that reads
one JSON field. That is uncomfortable and it is exactly the point of
preregistration: the primary metric got audited **before** any training run
produced a number that anyone would want to defend. The mechanism to fix it
ships dormant and byte-identical until a rubric opts in, the migration lands as a
declared instrument revision, and the frontier rows and teacher pilot say what
the environment is for — measuring everyone, and buying trajectories good enough
to train on. One codebase, one verification spine, now with the spine itself
under test.
